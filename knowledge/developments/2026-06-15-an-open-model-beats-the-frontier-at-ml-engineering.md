Weco AI co-founder Zhengyao Jiang reported benchmarking seven frontier models on three categories of autoresearch task, ML engineering, harness/prompt engineering and algorithmic discovery, with Fable-5 winning overall even under a cost constraint and the open [Kimi-K2.7-Code](/systems/kimi-k2-7-code.md) surpassing every frontier model on ML engineering ([post](https://x.com/zhengyaojiang/status/2066213302921802194)). The winner, [Claude Fable 5](/systems/claude-fable-5.md), had just been pulled from public access by US export controls, so the [benchmark](/benchmarks/weco-autoresearch-benchmark.md) crowned a model nobody outside Anthropic could run while an open Chinese model held the track closest to the recursive loop. It reads as a measurement of who can do the ML engineering that [recursive self-improvement](/themes/recursive-self-improvement.md) depends on: it follows [PostTrainBench](/developments/2026-03-12-posttrainbench-v1.md)'s test of whether agents can post-train themselves and Anthropic's [52x training-code speedup](/developments/2026-06-05-fifty-two-x-where-a-human-reaches-four.md), and precedes Weco's own [first evidence of consistent recursive self-improvement](/developments/2026-07-15-the-first-evidence-of-consistent-recursive-self-improvement.md) a month later.
